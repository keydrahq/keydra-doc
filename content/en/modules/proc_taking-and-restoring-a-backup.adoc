= Taking a backup and restoring one

.Prerequisites

* `transfer:export` on the target to take one, `transfer:import` to restore.
* A destination exists.

.Procedure

To take one now:

. Open the target and go to *Backups*.
. Choose the *Destination*.
. Click *Back up now*.
. Optionally narrow with *Keys matching*, set a *File name* prefix, and choose *Keep the
  last* _n_.
. Confirm.

To restore one:

. On the same page, find the file in the list. Each row shows *File*, *Size*, *Written*,
  *Taken from* and *Taken at*.
. Click *Restore*.
. Decide whether to *Overwrite keys that are already there*. This is off by default: a
  restore usually goes into a store that has moved on since, and quietly overwriting the
  newer data is the one outcome nobody wants by default.
. If the destination seals to a key, supply the *Private key*. It is used for this restore
  and kept nowhere.
. Confirm.

image::target-backups.jpg["A target’s Backups tab with no destination configured yet"]

.Verification

Taking one reports *Backed up as {name}* with how many keys, how large, and how many older
files were removed.

Restoring reports *{restored} written, {skipped} already there, {failed} refused.*.

[IMPORTANT]
====
If the file came from a different target, {product-name} says so before restoring: *It was
taken from <from> and you are restoring it into <into>.* That is sometimes exactly right —
copying production into staging — and sometimes the mistake nobody notices until later.
====
